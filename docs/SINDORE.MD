# OPERATION SINDOOR

## 1. Background

A terrorist attack occurred in Pahalgam on 22 April 2025.

The attack killed 26 people.

India identified Pakistan-based terrorist organizations as responsible for the attack.

India decided to conduct a military response.

The name of the response was **Operation Sindoor**.

The operation started during the night of 6 May and 7 May 2025.

The word *Sindoor* refers to the red vermilion that many married Hindu women wear.

The Government of India connected the name with the women who lost their husbands in the Pahalgam attack.

---

## 2. Initial Objective

The initial objective was to destroy terrorist infrastructure.

The initial objective was not to attack the Pakistani military.

Indian agencies collected intelligence about terrorist facilities.

The agencies identified nine locations as targets.

The Indian Armed Forces selected the targets after an intelligence assessment.

The forces did not include Pakistani military installations in the initial target list.

---

## 3. Initial Targets

India identified nine terrorist infrastructure locations.

The targets were:

1. Sawai Nala at Muzaffarabad.
2. Syedna Bilal at Muzaffarabad.
3. Gulpur at Kotli.
4. Barnala at Bhimber.
5. Markaz Abbas at Kotli.
6. Sarjal near Sialkot.
7. Mehmoona Joya near Sialkot.
8. Markaz Taiba at Muridke.
9. Markaz Subhan Allah at Bahawalpur.

India associated these locations with Lashkar-e-Taiba, Jaish-e-Mohammed, and Hizbul Mujahideen.

Markaz Taiba at Muridke was an important Lashkar-e-Taiba facility.

Markaz Subhan Allah at Bahawalpur was an important Jaish-e-Mohammed facility.

---

## 4. Initial Strike

The Indian Armed Forces used precision weapons for the initial attack.

Aircraft did not have to fly directly above the targets.

The aircraft could release weapons from a safe distance.

This type of attack is called a **stand-off attack**.

The strike package included Rafale fighter aircraft.

It also included SCALP cruise missiles and HAMMER precision-guided weapons.

The forces used intelligence data to select the targets.

The forces launched the weapons against the selected targets.

Intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance systems then assessed the strike results.

The simplified sequence was:

**Intelligence → Target Selection → Weapon Selection → Stand-off Launch → Precision Strike → Damage Assessment**

The purpose was to destroy the selected terrorist infrastructure and control further escalation.

---

## 5. Change in the Operational Situation

The operational situation changed after the first strikes.

The threat to Indian military installations increased.

India detected drones and missiles that moved toward several military locations.

These locations included Jammu, Srinagar, Pathankot, Amritsar, Adampur, Bathinda, Chandigarh, and Bhuj.

Indian air-defence systems responded to these threats.

The Integrated Counter-UAS Grid detected and engaged unmanned aerial threats.

Indian air-defence systems also intercepted incoming threats.

---

## 6. Attack on Air-Defence Systems

India then expanded the operation.

Indian forces attacked hostile air-defence and radar systems.

The purpose was to reduce the threat to Indian aircraft and other military assets.

Indian forces neutralized an air-defence system near Lahore.

This action changed the type of the operation.

The operation was no longer limited to terrorist infrastructure.

The operation now included hostile military systems that could affect Indian operations.

---

## 7. Large Drone Threat

A large drone threat occurred during the night of 8 May and 9 May.

India detected drone activity at 36 locations.

The area extended from Leh in the north to Sir Creek in the west.

Approximately 300 to 400 drones were involved.

Indian forces used different systems against these drones.

They used kinetic systems.

A kinetic system physically destroys a target.

They also used electronic and other non-kinetic systems.

These systems can interfere with the control, navigation, or communication of a drone.

Indian forces recovered parts from some drones.

The preliminary examination identified some drones as Turkish-origin ASISGUARD SONGAR systems.

---

## 8. Indian Counter-Drone Response

India responded to the drone threat.

Indian forces launched armed drones against hostile air-defence locations.

The forces attacked four air-defence locations.

India destroyed one radar system.

The operation now included several types of military systems at the same time.

These systems included:

- Fighter aircraft.
- Precision missiles.
- Armed drones.
- Loitering munitions.
- Counter-drone systems.
- Radar systems.
- Surface-to-air missile systems.
- Electronic warfare systems.
- Artillery.
- Command-and-control systems.

India used Akash, Pechora, and OSA-AK air-defence systems.

India also used low-level air-defence guns.

Integrated counter-UAS systems provided additional protection.

---

## 9. Electronic Warfare

Electronic warfare was an important part of the operation.

Electronic warfare systems detected and disrupted hostile electronic activity.

These systems helped protect Indian military assets.

They also supported the detection and defeat of drones.

Electronic warfare worked with radar, air-defence, aircraft, and command systems.

This created a layered defence network.

---

## 10. Escalation on 9–10 May

The operation expanded further during 9 May and 10 May.

India detected additional attacks against Indian military installations.

India then attacked important military infrastructure.

The targets included:

- Airbases.
- Radar systems.
- Command-and-control facilities.
- Weapons-storage facilities.
- Other military support infrastructure.

The purpose was to reduce the ability of hostile forces to conduct additional attacks.

---

## 11. Airbase Strikes

Indian forces attacked several important airbases and military facilities.

These included facilities at:

- Noor Khan.
- Rafiqui.
- Murid.
- Sargodha.
- Rahim Yar Khan.
- Jacobabad.
- Sukkur.
- Bholari.

Precision weapons were used against selected military targets.

BrahMos was one of the weapon systems used during Operation Sindoor.

The attacks damaged important military infrastructure.

---

## 12. Command and Control

Operation Sindoor required continuous command and control.

Commanders had to monitor many conditions at the same time.

They had to know the status of available aircraft.

They had to know the weapons available on each aircraft.

They had to know the location and condition of hostile radar systems.

They had to know the location of surface-to-air missile systems.

They had to monitor drone activity.

They had to monitor Indian air-defence systems.

They also had to monitor electronic warfare activity.

The threat situation changed continuously.

Therefore, planners had to update mission plans continuously.

---

## 13. Air-Operations Planning

For each mission, planners had to consider several parameters.

These parameters included:

- Aircraft availability.
- Aircraft fuel.
- Aircraft range.
- Weapon load.
- Weapon range.
- Target type.
- Target priority.
- Target location.
- Hostile radar coverage.
- Surface-to-air missile coverage.
- Drone activity.
- Friendly air-defence coverage.
- Electronic warfare support.
- Civilian airspace restrictions.
- Weather.
- Alternate routes.
- Alternate targets.
- Mission risk.

A change in one parameter could change the mission plan.

For example, a new radar threat could make a planned route unsafe.

The planner then had to select another route or another weapon.

A change in target priority could require another aircraft.

A change in aircraft availability could require a new weapon and mission assignment.

## 15. End of Major Military Action

On 10 May 2025, the Pakistani Director General of Military Operations contacted the Indian Director General of Military Operations.

The two military commanders discussed the military situation.

They agreed to stop firing and military action.

The cessation applied to operations on land, at sea, and in the air.

The arrangement started at 17:00 IST on 10 May 2025.

Large-scale military action then stopped.

---

## 16. Main Technical Characteristics

Operation Sindoor was not one isolated air strike.

It became a multi-domain military operation.

The operation combined:

**Air power + precision weapons + air defence + drones + counter-drone systems + radar + electronic warfare + artillery + ISR + command and control.**

The systems had to operate as one network.

Information had to move rapidly between sensors, commanders, aircraft, weapon systems, and air-defence units.

The operational environment also changed rapidly.

This increased the complexity of mission planning.

---

## 17. Important Planning Lesson

Operation Sindoor shows the importance of real-time decision support in modern air operations.

A commander cannot consider aircraft availability alone.

The commander must consider many connected parameters.

For example:

**Aircraft + Crew + Weapon + Target + Route + Threat + Air Defence + Weather + Time + Mission Priority**

All these parameters can change during an operation.

A decision-support system must collect the latest information.

It must identify conflicts.

It must calculate possible mission options.

It must show the risks of each option.

It must help the commander select a suitable course of action.

The final decision remains with the military commander.
